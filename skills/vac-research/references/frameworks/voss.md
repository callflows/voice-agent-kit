# Chris Voss — Never Split the Difference

## Core Concept
Tactical empathy and calibrated questions. Negotiation techniques adapted for sales conversations.

## Key Techniques

### Labeling
Name the other person's emotion to validate it and build rapport.
- "It sounds like you've got a lot on your plate right now."
- "It seems like you've had a bad experience with this before."
- Formula: "It sounds like... / It seems like..." (never "I think you feel...")

### Mirroring
Repeat the last 1-3 words of what the other person said. Creates connection and encourages them to elaborate.
- Prospect: "We don't have a need for that." → Agent: "No need for that?"

### Calibrated Questions
Open-ended "How" and "What" questions that give the other person the illusion of control.
- "What would the ideal next step look like for you?"
- "How does your process for that work today?"

### Accusation Audit
Preemptively address negatives the prospect might be thinking.
- "You're probably thinking: here we go, another one of these calls."
- "You're probably wondering why us."

## Best For
- Supporting technique alongside any primary framework
- Building rapport in the first 20% of the call (Gong research)
- De-escalating tense situations
- Getting prospects to open up

## Not Ideal For
- As primary framework (it's a toolbox, not a conversation structure)
- Overuse of labeling feels manipulative
- Mirroring in very short calls (no time to elaborate)

## Key Principles for Voice Agents
- Use Labeling sparingly (1-2 per call max, or it feels therapeutic)
- Mirroring works brilliantly in voice — natural and conversational
- Accusation Audit is powerful for outbound openers (disarm resistance)
- Calibrated Questions > closed questions (always)
